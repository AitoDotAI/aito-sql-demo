The climax: the same database refutes its own first answer

Three queries in one session — or three clicks in the explorer, which is the same thing. The naive answer is not a strawman; it is what a dashboard would conclude.

beat 3 of 4
01 · THE MARGINAL
Which channel produces churners?
distributor-ME churns at ×1.91 — far above every other channel. Every dashboard in the company shows this, and it is true.
→ "cut the ME budget"
02 · CONDITIONED
Add climate = hot to the context.
The channel's lift collapses to ×1.06 while cooling = passive holds at ×2.04. The channel was never the cause; it just sells into hot regions. The viewer does this themselves — one chip.
→ "sell the active SKU there"
03 · GROUND TRUTH
We wrote the generator.
We planted passive×hot at 2.10× and gave the channel no direct effect. The database recovered 2.04× from a column four hops away, with no feature engineering and no model.
→ correction confirmed

Read M1 and M3 together. M1 is nearly invisible marginally (1.28×) and only appears once conditioned; M3 is the reverse — loud marginally, gone once conditioned. That pair is the entire argument for looking across the whole chain instead of one dashboard at a time.

On causation. relate() returns lift — association, not causation. The only reason cause can be discussed here is that we wrote the generator and know the ground truth. Conditioning on observed confounds is not causal identification: this screens candidates and flags which survive controlling for what was measured. The data is synthetic and its causal structure is planted by construction; no claim of causal inference on real-world data is made anywhere.
